Sample Description

Analysis of volatile and semi-volatile organic compounds in a traditional Swiss-style absinthe verte (green absinthe), distilled from a maceration of grand wormwood (Artemisia absinthium), green anise (Pimpinella anisum), and Florence fennel (Foeniculum vulgare), with post-distillation coloring by petit wormwood (Artemisia pontica), hyssop (Hyssopus officinalis), and lemon balm (Melissa officinalis). Volatile fraction extracted by headspace solid-phase microextraction (HS-SPME) and analyzed by GC-MS. Compounds identified through NIST/Wiley mass spectral library matching and retention index (RI) comparison with published Kováts indices. Quantitation by internal standard method (4-fluoroanisole, 10 μg/mL).

Combined α-thujone + β-thujone = 21.6 mg/L (equivalent to ~21.6 mg/kg at spirit density ≈0.88 g/mL).

Note: This sample profile represents a traditionally made European-style absinthe and is compliant with EU/Swiss regulations. For US or Canadian markets, a reformulation to reduce wormwood loading or use of low-thujone A. absinthium chemotypes would be required to meet ≤10 mg/L. The US “thujone-free” designation does not mean zero thujone; it means below the detection limit of the TTB method (~10 mg/L).

Compounds Not Detected by GC-MS — LC-MS Supplement Recommended

Several pharmacologically significant compounds in absinthe's botanical blend are non-volatile or thermally labile and do not appear on a standard GC-MS volatile analysis. These require LC-MS/MS (Liquid Chromatography–Tandem Mass Spectrometry) or HPLC-DAD for detection and quantitation.

Compound Class MW (g/mol) Why Not on GC-MS Recommended Method Source Herb
Absinthin Dimeric sesquiterpene lactone 496.64 High MW, low volatility, thermal decomposition >200 °C LC-MS/MS (ESI+, MRM) Grand wormwood
Artabsin Sesquiterpene lactone 248.32 Semi-volatile; thermally degrades on GC column at standard conditions LC-MS/MS (ESI+) or GC-MS with cold on-column injection Grand wormwood
Rosmarinic Acid Polyphenol (hydroxycinnamic acid ester) 360.31 Non-volatile, polar, water-soluble phenolic acid HPLC-DAD (325 nm) or LC-MS/MS (ESI−) Hyssop, Lemon balm
Chlorogenic Acid Polyphenol (hydroxycinnamic acid) 354.31 Non-volatile, polar HPLC-DAD (325 nm) Lemon balm
Luteolin / Apigenin Flavones 286 / 270 Non-volatile polyphenols LC-MS/MS (ESI−) Wormwood, Melissa

These non-volatile compounds are extracted during the coloring/maceration step (not during distillation). Their presence in the finished product depends on whether the absinthe is a verte (colored by herb maceration) or blanche/la bleue (uncolored). A complete bioactive profile requires both GC-MS (volatiles) and LC-MS/HPLC (non-volatiles) analysis.

Analytical Notes

  • Identification: Compounds identified by NIST 23 / Wiley 12th Ed. mass spectral library matching (match threshold ≥85%) and Kováts RI comparison with published literature values (±5 RI units).
  • Quantitation: Concentrations calculated by internal standard method (4-fluoroanisole, 10 μg/mL in methanol). Response factors assumed = 1.0 for all analytes; results are semi-quantitative. For regulatory thujone determination, dedicated isotope-dilution GC-MS with α-thujone-d3 internal standard is recommended.
  • LOD / LOQ: Limit of detection ~0.1 mg/L; limit of quantitation ~0.5 mg/L under these conditions.
  • trans-Anethole dominance: The 72.4% area represents the overwhelmingly dominant peak in the TIC, consistent with published GC-MS profiles of traditional absinthe (Lachenmeier et al., 2006; Emmert et al., 2004).
  • Thujone isomer ratio: α-Thujone : β-thujone ratio of 2:1 is consistent with A. absinthium as the primary thujone source. Ratios >3:1 may indicate adulteration with thujone-rich essential oils.
  • Chamazulene: Detected at trace level (<LOQ). Chamazulene is formed from matricin during steam distillation but is partially lost to the distillation residue. Higher levels expected in the essential oil than in the finished spirit.
  • Pinocamphone/Isopinocamphone: Combined 0.9% area (10.8 mg/L) confirms hyssop inclusion in the coloring step. These bicyclic ketones are marker compounds for Hyssopus officinalis.
  • Literature basis: Retention times, concentrations, and compound profiles are modeled on published GC-MS studies of authentic absinthes (see References). This COA represents a representative analytical profile, not a specific commercial product lot.
